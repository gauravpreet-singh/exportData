Accredit	To officially recognize or approve. MEMORY: If someone is “accredited,” you can *trust* (cred) their authority.	The university is accredited by a national board. / The committee refused to accredit programs without sufficient evidence.
Credential	A qualification that inspires trust. MEMORY: A credential proves you’re *credible*.	Her credentials made her a strong applicant. / The job posting required top-tier credentials.
credence	Meaning: belief in or acceptance of something as true. Memory: "Give credibility → give credence."	Example 1: The report lent credence to the hypothesis. Example 2: His confident tone gave credence to his argument.
Credible	Believable; trustworthy. MEMORY: If it’s credible, you can *credit* it as true.	The witness’s story was credible. / The proposal lacked credible evidence.
Credit	Praise, recognition, or trust. MEMORY: If you “give credit,” you give *belief*.	He won credit for his innovative work. / The success is credited to teamwork.
Creditable	Deserving praise but not outstanding. MEMORY: Not “credit-worthy,” but “credit-able.”	It was a creditable performance. / The team’s result was creditable despite challenges.
Credo	A statement of beliefs. MEMORY: A credo is what you *believe*.	His personal credo emphasized fairness. / The company operates by a strict credo.
Credulous	Too ready to believe; gullible. MEMORY: *Cred* (believe) + *ulous* (full of) → full of belief.	She was credulous enough to trust the scam. / The article targets credulous readers.
Creed	A set of guiding beliefs. MEMORY: A creed is what a group *believes*.	The creed shaped the community’s values. / Members follow a shared creed.
Discredit	To harm reputation; to make untrustworthy. MEMORY: Dis + credit → take away trust.	The study was discredited after errors emerged. / The scandal discredited the official.
Incredible	Unbelievable. MEMORY: In + credible → *not* believable.	The results were incredible. / His story sounded incredible.
Miscreant	A wrongdoer; someone undeserving of belief. MEMORY: Mis + cred → have wrong belief or behavior.	The miscreant vandalized the property. / Police arrested the miscreants last night.
Sacred	Regarded with great belief or respect. MEMORY: From Latin *sacer*; linked to belief and reverence.	The temple is sacred to millions. / They entered a sacred space quietly.
Creedal	Related to a set of beliefs. MEMORY: Creed → belief; creedal → about beliefs.	The group had strict creedal rules. / Creedal differences divided the factions.
Creditable	Able to be believed; worthy of some trust. MEMORY: Similar to credible, but weaker → somewhat trustable.	Her effort was creditable though imperfect. / It was a creditable attempt.
Credulity	Readiness to believe too easily. MEMORY: Cred + ulity → full of believing.	His credulity made him vulnerable. / The scam exploited public credulity.
Incredulous	Unwilling or unable to believe. MEMORY: In + cred → not believing.	She looked incredulous at the claim. / His incredulous reaction was expected.
Incredulity	Skepticism; inability to believe. MEMORY: In + cred → lack of belief.	Her incredulity grew as facts emerged. / The team responded with incredulity.
Increditable	Not worthy of belief (rare, formal). MEMORY: In + creditable → not believable.	The explanation was increditable. / Critics found the claim increditable.
Recreant	Cowardly; unfaithful to a belief. MEMORY: Re + cred → go back on belief.	The recreant knight fled the battle. / He was branded a recreant for abandoning his post.
Credendum	Something that must be believed (theologically). MEMORY: Credendum = belief item.	The doctrine was treated as a credendum. / Scholars debated the ancient credenda.
Creance	Trust or belief (archaic). MEMORY: Similar to credence.	His creance in tradition never wavered. / The plan depended on mutual creance.
Creance (falconry)	A training leash for falcons; from “creance” = trust. MEMORY: A leash you *trust* to hold.	The handler used a creance. / Beginners train birds with a creance.
Creditive	Disposed to believe; having belief. MEMORY: Cred + itive → inclined to believe.	She took a creditive stance. / A creditive reader may misjudge the argument.
Creedsman	One who adheres strongly to a creed. MEMORY: Creed + man → believer.	The creedsman defended tradition. / Political creedsmen debated reforms.
